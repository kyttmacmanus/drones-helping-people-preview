COMMUNITIES

Mapping in relationship with people and place

On Staten Island and in Mtukuzi, aerial mapping took place with community partners and local collaborators. The work connects questions about buildings, waterfronts, agriculture and water with field observations that people can examine together. Each place has its own relationships, questions and record of follow-up.

STATEN ISLAND · 2023–PRESENT

From 60 Hannah Street to the North Shore

SDEV4650, Building Climate Justice, brought students into a community partnership on Staten Island. The sequence extends from a historic firehouse survey to work on sharing student maps, then to continued fieldwork at North Shore Esplanade.

CIESIN · Columbia University · SDEV4650 · Staten Island Urban Center and community partners. North Shore fieldwork also forms part of EFPL's NYC Parks work.

Course fieldwork

Collaboratory funding for SDEV4650 included a drone purchase. The December 2023 fieldwork at 60 Hannah Street produced mapped imagery and a photogrammetric reconstruction of the historic firehouse, connecting course methods with community-defined waterfront questions.

Access for the partner

January 2024 technical support focused on making the student StoryMap and map layers accessible to the Staten Island Urban Center. Sharing the work was part of the contribution. Subsequent use in planning has not been evaluated here.

North Shore continuation

The December 1, 2025 EFPL flight at North Shore Esplanade produced imagery and elevation products for examining waterfront conditions. The community partnership and the NYC Parks survey are simultaneous contexts for the work.

From the field session to mapped information

The firehouse orthomosaic records the earlier site. The North Shore imagery and terrain display offer complementary views of the later waterfront survey. Each image links to its source and interpretation limits in the atlas.

MTUKUZI, EASTERN ZAMBIA · JUNE 2026

Local collaboration, selected sites and field observations

Work with University of Zambia colleagues and local partners on June 13, 2026 documented selected village and school areas, agricultural sites, a river reach and a water-well structure. The imagery provides a record for inquiry about these places; the mapped sites do not constitute coverage of the wider catchment.

EFPL + CIESIN · Columbia University · University of Zambia colleagues and local partners

People and local context

Field colleagues and local partners contributed the setting in which mapping took place. The photographs document that collaboration. Individual responsibilities and participant names are included only where source records establish them.

Observation across several sites

The retained imagery records buildings and paths, cultivated land, visible river banks and a water-well structure. These are different subjects with different interpretation needs; their presence in an image does not establish agricultural performance, water safety or water availability.

Maps for interpretation

Individual camera frames retain the acquisition view. Orthomosaics combine overlapping photographs into spatial displays. They support inspection of visible features, with coverage, timing and independent accuracy read from the accompanying records.

A learning follow-up

Raphael Sutiono and Kytt MacManus developed a contribution for TOPSTSCHOOL around organizing drone data, interpreting products and connecting them with local follow-up. The teaching contribution extends the field record; measured community effects remain a separate question.

SHARED METHODS, DISTINCT CONTEXTS

Reading the contribution with its evidence

Field participation

Working-group photographs document activities and equipment in place. They help explain the collaboration; evaluated learning or community benefit requires additional evidence.

Photogrammetric products

Overlapping camera images produce orthomosaics and surface reconstructions. Pixel spacing, geometric detail and processing residuals do not substitute for independent accuracy assessment.

Access and follow-up

Partner access, interpretation and subsequent use are parts of the record to document with collaborators. The portfolio distinguishes completed fieldwork and outputs from intended applications.
