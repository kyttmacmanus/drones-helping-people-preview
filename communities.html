COMMUNITIES

Community-engaged mapping

Community partners and local collaborators worked with students and researchers on mapping in Staten Island and around Mtukuzi. The projects address buildings, waterfronts, agriculture and water infrastructure. Each has its own collaborators, questions and follow-up.

STATEN ISLAND · 2023–PRESENT

From 60 Hannah Street to the North Shore

SDEV4650, Building Climate Justice, brought students into a community partnership on Staten Island. Students mapped a historic firehouse, shared their maps with partners and later continued fieldwork at North Shore Esplanade.

CIESIN · Columbia University · SDEV4650 · Staten Island Urban Center and community partners. North Shore fieldwork also forms part of EFPL's NYC Parks work.

Course fieldwork

Collaboratory funding for SDEV4650 included a drone purchase. The December 2023 fieldwork at 60 Hannah Street produced mapped imagery and a photogrammetric reconstruction of the historic firehouse, connecting course methods with community-defined waterfront questions.

Sharing maps with SIUC

January 2024 technical support focused on making the student StoryMap and map layers accessible to the Staten Island Urban Center. Sharing the work was part of the contribution. Subsequent use in planning has not been evaluated here.

North Shore continuation

The December 1, 2025 EFPL flight at North Shore Esplanade produced imagery and elevation products for examining waterfront conditions. The survey contributes to both the community partnership and NYC Parks research.

Mapped products

The firehouse orthomosaic records the earlier site. The North Shore imagery and terrain display offer complementary views of the later waterfront survey. Open an image for its date, credit and interpretation limits in the atlas.

MTUKUZI, EASTERN ZAMBIA · JUNE 2026

Selected-site mapping around Mtukuzi

University of Zambia colleagues and local partners mapped selected village and school areas, agricultural sites, a river reach and a water-well structure on June 13, 2026. These observations cover selected sites rather than the wider catchment.

EFPL + CIESIN · Columbia University · University of Zambia colleagues and local partners

Local collaboration

Local partners and field colleagues contributed local knowledge and worked together during the mapping. Photographs document the field activity; they do not establish individual responsibilities.

Observation across several sites

Imagery records buildings, paths, cultivated land, river banks and a water-well structure. It does not establish crop performance, water safety or water availability.

Maps for interpretation

Camera frames show individual viewpoints; orthomosaics combine overlapping photographs. Dates, mapped coverage and accuracy limits accompany the images.

A learning follow-up

Raphael Sutiono and Kytt MacManus developed a contribution for TOPSTSCHOOL around organizing drone data, interpreting products and connecting them with local follow-up. Community effects were not evaluated here.

SHARED METHODS, DISTINCT CONTEXTS

Interpreting the mapping

Field participation

Working-group photographs document activities and equipment in place. They help explain the collaboration; evaluated learning or community benefit requires additional evidence.

Photogrammetric products

Overlapping camera images produce orthomosaics and surface reconstructions. Pixel spacing, geometric detail and processing residuals do not substitute for independent accuracy assessment.

Access and follow-up

Partner access, interpretation and subsequent use are parts of the record to document with collaborators. Completed fieldwork and products are distinguished from proposed uses.
