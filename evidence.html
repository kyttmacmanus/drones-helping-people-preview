OBSERVATION, RECONSTRUCTION & INTERPRETATION

Data products, methods and sources

Drone imagery offers detailed observations of a place. Its scientific value depends on how the data were acquired, processed and evaluated, and on the questions people bring to them.

DATA & PRODUCTS

Different products answer different questions.

The portfolio combines workshop and field photographs with camera observations, mapping products and photogrammetric reconstructions. Captions identify the product type, collection date, project and source. The atlas displays selected web derivatives; native georeferenced data and processing records provide the basis for analysis.

01

Field photographs

Working groups, equipment and field settings explain how an activity took place. Photographs alone cannot establish learning gains or subsequent community outcomes.

02

Camera observations

Individual visible-light and thermal frames record a particular time and viewpoint. Thermal colors here are qualitative where calibration has not been established.

03

Orthomosaics & elevation

Overlapping images can be processed into georeferenced composites and surface models. Ground control, independent checkpoints and appropriate acquisition conditions are needed to evaluate their accuracy.

04

Point clouds & meshes

Photogrammetric points and textured surfaces describe reconstructed scene geometry. A point-cloud file or a three-dimensional display does not by itself establish LiDAR acquisition or bare-earth terrain.

SCIENTIFIC INTERPRETATION

Detail, accuracy and explanation are different.

Terms and limits used throughout the portfolio
Term or productWhat it describesWhat requires further evidence
Ground sampling distance (GSD)The ground dimension represented by an image pixel. Output raster pixel spacing is a related processing parameter.Independent horizontal or vertical positional accuracy.
OrthomosaicA georeferenced composite made from overlapping photographs after geometric processing.Accurate alignment and dimensions across gaps, edges or weakly reconstructed areas.
DSM / DTMA digital surface model represents visible surfaces, including structures and vegetation. A digital terrain model estimates the underlying terrain.Bare-earth classification and independently tested elevation accuracy; a DTM label alone is insufficient.
GCP / checkpointA ground-control point supplies surveyed control to processing. An independent checkpoint is reserved for evaluating positional accuracy.Accuracy beyond the tested locations, conditions and reference measurements.
Point cloud / LASA collection of three-dimensional points. LAS is a point-cloud file format that can contain photogrammetric points.The acquisition technology, terrain classification and measurement uncertainty.
Thermal displayRelative apparent surface-temperature patterns in the displayed sensor observation.Calibrated temperatures, material properties or physiological interpretations.
Terrain and environmental processesSurface geometry that can inform questions about airflow, runoff or site conditions.Validated wind or hydrologic dynamics, engineering design and demonstrated environmental outcomes.
Rendered view or screenshotA visual presentation of a product, sometimes using perspective, color scales or vertical exaggeration.Analysis-ready georeferenced values, accurate dimensions or the complete underlying dataset.

From useful observations to demonstrated outcomes

Mapping can support teaching, research questions and discussion with partners. A proposed benefit remains an aim until its use and effects are evaluated. Local knowledge, field checks and appropriate measurements are needed to move from visible patterns to explanations or decisions.

A FIELD EXAMPLE: MTUKUZI

Five capture missions. Several processing contexts.

On June 13, 2026, village and school, agricultural, river and water-well sites near Mtukuzi were documented at different scales. These five capture missions cover selected sites. The six Site Scan processing records, including a combined run, describe processing contexts rather than six independent field acquisitions.

Relating the crop captures to processed products

The broader Crops capture is linked to the processed Crops-2 products; the lower-altitude Crops-2 capture is linked to the processed Crops products. This crosswalk follows source-image footprint, processed extent and resolution. It remains an interpretation to confirm against the original processing manifest.

Capture GSD and processed pixel spacing are different fields. Neither supplies an independent accuracy estimate.

Keep each processing run with its own metrics

A combined Mtukuzi run calibrated 515 of 979 images and reported average camera GSD of 0.016 m. Its report states that ground-control points and independent checkpoints were not used. Those metrics describe that combined run and do not automatically apply to the five companion missions or establish independent accuracy.

RESEARCH CONTEXT

Terrain observations support further investigation.

NYC PARKS RESEARCH

Map the landscape, then evaluate change.

Four Sparrow Marsh, North Shore Esplanade and Morningside Park provide different settings for studying surface form, shorelines, vegetation and water-related questions. Repeat mapping requires comparable coverage, processing, reference control and uncertainty estimates before observed differences can be interpreted as environmental change.

LAMONT / EFPL

Geometry for questions about airflow.

Detailed surface geometry can help represent terrain, vegetation and structures in wind-dynamics research associated with EFPL's ARO project. Evaluating airflow requires appropriate observations and model validation in addition to the geometry.

The Lamont interactive view samples source LAS tiles from a photogrammetric reconstruction. It illustrates visible scene structure and makes no independent positional-accuracy or bare-earth claim.
