2025–present

Repeated mapping at Four Sparrow Marsh

EFPL and Natan Charytan use repeated drone surveys to document conditions at Four Sparrow Marsh for ecological and restoration research.

Question and project details
Question
How can repeated spatial observations support study of a restored marsh?
Contribution
Permitted drone surveys, orthomosaics, elevation products and 3D surface views.

The April 18, 2025 operation was the first UAS flight approved by NYC Parks, according to Kytt MacManus’s first-hand project account. Wind curtailed that operation; the team returned on May 2. Repeat surveys document conditions, but interpreting change requires aligned observations and independent accuracy checks.

November 16, 2026

Highbridge Park aerial mapping and 3D documentation

On November 16, 2026, permitted DJI survey flights mapped Highbridge Park in northern Manhattan. A Skydio mission photographed the water tower and nearby terrain at closer range.

Processing produced orthomosaics, surface and terrain products, point clouds and a textured reconstruction of the tower area. These products document the park’s steep, wooded terrain and built structures.

The work extends mapping at Four Sparrow Marsh and Morningside Park and provides another setting for testing aerial mapping and 3D documentation in NYC Parks.

Lamont terrain and wind research

EFPL uses terrain and surface geometry to study wind observations and airflow modeling at Lamont.

Question and project details
Question
How do terrain, vegetation and structures affect local wind observations?
Contribution
Mapped surfaces and field instrumentation used alongside meteorological and Doppler LiDAR observations.

DURIP supported the IF1200A purchase for an ARO project. The interactive Lamont point sample is photogrammetric surface reconstruction. A separate March 24, 2026 LiDAR view uses relative coordinates because its LAS CRS label conflicts with coordinate magnitudes. Neither display establishes independent accuracy or validates a wind model.

EFPL · Columbia Engineering · DURIP-supported instrumentation · ARO research

Ongoing

SAMARA: aerial logistics for electrification

Zambia fieldwork connected Kytt MacManus with Matthew Orosz and collaborators working on rural electrification in southern Africa. Kytt later joined Team SAMARA, which is developing a heavy-lift uncrewed aircraft concept for remote payload delivery and utility operations.

Team SAMARA participated in the DARPA Lift Challenge at the National Museum of the U.S. Air Force in Dayton, Ohio, in August 2026. The team reached spin-up testing, which informed subsequent prototype development.

The team continues prototype development of simpler, more durable aircraft configurations for possible use in electrification and infrastructure logistics in Africa.

October 28, 2017

Black Rock Forest balloon mapping

Students from three Columbia GIS courses used low-cost balloon cameras, GPS and mobile field observations at Black Rock Forest.

Question and project details
Question
How can students combine balloon imagery with ground observations?
Contribution
Approximately 35 students worked with Public Lab balloon kits, true-color and modified infrared cameras, GPS and Collector for ArcGIS. Tree observations included hemlock woolly adelgid indicators and diameter measurements.

An Earth Institute Course Support Grant funded the Fall 2017 visit. Kytt MacManus, Dara Mendeloff and Greg Yetman instructed the courses, with teaching assistants Sara Lytle and Christina Paton; Gina Dinnegan helped organize the trip. Students used Mobius Action Cams with true-color and modified infrared cameras at the Upper Reservoir. High winds limited the balloon operation, but the group collected usable images and ground observations. This work preceded the later drone-teaching program.

March 11, 2025

William Paterson tree mapping

Nicole Davi hosted a workshop that combined campus tree observations in ArcGIS Field Maps with drone imagery and elevation products.

Question and project details
Question
How can field tree observations be compared with aerial imagery and elevation products?
Contribution
Field GIS, visible-light and thermal observations, and Drone2Map products. A public web map includes the spatial products and tree-observation layer.

After the March 11 workshop, Kytt shared visible-light and thermal imagery, Skydio 3D imagery, processed products and teaching materials. Elevation products were identified as potentially useful for tree identification. The public map includes true orthomosaics, DSM/DTM, relief and contours, plus tree observations. These products do not establish a complete tree census, species-identification accuracy or calibrated thermal temperatures. The product views show imagery on a neutral background.

April 15, 2026

Hemlock Hill field instrumentation

SDEV3450 students and Watershed Agricultural Council partners practiced ground observations, positioning, photogrammetry and LiDAR field methods at Hemlock Hill.

Question and project details
Question
How can ground measurements and aerial sensors be used together in fieldwork?
Contribution
GNSS and ground-control observations, total-station measurements, Mavic 3 photogrammetry, IF1200A LiDAR acquisition, multispectral camera work and data processing.

The April 15 field session combined student and partner work with several instruments. Processed photogrammetric products include an orthomosaic, DSM, DTM, point cloud and mesh; these are separate from the IF1200A LiDAR acquisition. Camera colors are shown without treating them as calibrated NDVI. The displays do not establish agricultural outcomes, independent checkpoint accuracy or a complete farm inventory.
