Mtukuzi data:

Raphael Sutiono and Kytt MacManus developed a working module using the Mtukuzi field campaign to teach drone-image interpretation, data organization, and documented handover. The activities connect spatial observations with questions for local investigation.

From Drone Imagery to Agricultural Decisions in Mtukuzi, Zambia
TOPS Community Module · Draft 5 · Prepared by Raphael Sutiono and Kytt MacManus

Explore the lesson ↓

THE GUIDING QUESTION

How can spatial information help us understand farming, water access, and livelihoods—and how should it be used and shared?

The capstone asks learners to document an observation, distinguish interpretation from evidence, and propose a follow-up question for discussion or field verification.

01 / THE LEARNING PATHWAY

Organize, interpret,
and document the data.

Draft 5 contains two lessons and an applied capstone. This page summarizes their structure and provides practice activities using selected companion products.

LESSON 1

Understand & organize

  1. Start with life and livelihoods in Mtukuzi.
  2. Identify source images, map products, and documentation.
  3. Complete metadata and plan a clear folder structure.

Document the package so another user can identify its contents, provenance, and missing information.

LESSON 2

Manage & hand over

  1. Choose a desktop, cloud, or hybrid workflow.
  2. Plan the transfer, backup, checks, and handover.

Evaluate connectivity, storage cost, access, and traceability when selecting a workflow.

APPLIED CAPSTONE

Investigate one question

  1. Use imagery, terrain, field context, and documentation to propose a focused follow-up.

Distinguish observations, tentative interpretations, and questions requiring additional evidence.

Read the data-quality note

Display previews are not native georeferenced rasters. The products show selected sites, with gaps and edge artifacts. “DTM” labeling alone does not verify bare-earth classification; imagery and terrain do not establish independent positional accuracy. Mesh renderings may use vertical exaggeration and should not be used for dimension checks.

A separate combined Mtukuzi processing report records 515 of 979 images calibrated and an average camera ground sampling distance (GSD) of 0.016 m. It explicitly says ground-control points and independent checkpoints were not used for that run. These statistics describe the combined run. The five companion missions have separate collection and processing records. Ground sampling distance and internal processing metrics do not provide independent positional-accuracy estimates.

04 / DATA MANAGEMENT AND HANDOVER

Select a data-management
workflow.

Compare desktop, cloud, and hybrid approaches in relation to storage, connectivity, collaboration, cost, and access.

A FIELD-TO-OFFICE HANDOVER

  1. Record the mission, date, and expected files.
  2. Keep source images and supporting records together.
  3. Check file counts, sizes, and a sample of readable files.
  4. Create a backup in a separate approved location.
  5. Record missing information, access conditions, and next steps.

The source lesson includes a simulated integrity check and a handover record for documenting the transfer.

05 / THE APPLIED CAPSTONE

Document an observation
and a follow-up question.

Use a selected image, a terrain example, and the accompanying source notes to complete a short observation record. Identify the evidence needed to evaluate the proposed interpretation.

The note is created in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, and this page does not store your answers.
