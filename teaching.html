STUDENTS, RESEARCH TRAINEES & EDUCATORS

Field instruction and aerial mapping

Students, educators and colleagues use aerial mapping and field observations to study spatial questions. Teaching includes balloon mapping at Black Rock Forest, drone fieldwork at Lamont, campus tree mapping, agricultural field methods and exchange with University of Zambia colleagues.

BUILDING TEACHING CAPACITY

Equipment support and field instruction

Teaching equipment

The 2021 Provost Emerging Technology Grant supported Drones and AR/VR for 3D Modeling in GIS, with $19,637 and CUIT support. CIESIN-based teaching connected that equipment and software capacity with SDEV3450 fieldwork, including the 2022 Callicoon exercise and student mapping.

Coursework with communities

SDEV4650, Building Climate Justice, connected field preparation with community-engaged spatial work. Collaboratory funding included a drone purchase. Lamont workshops supported the course's later Staten Island field activities.

Workshops with students and educators

Workshops extended to NSF research students and educators, William Paterson University and the New York State Earth Science Teachers Association. Kytt MacManus worked with course teams and colleagues across CIESIN and EFPL.

Participation does not by itself demonstrate learning gains; those require assessment.

Earlier GIS education with Earth2Class

The Earth2Class archive lists Kytt MacManus's GIS education workshops with Steve Kluge on April 18, 2009 and Malanding Jaiteh on September 26, 2009. These earlier educator workshops introduced geographic information and visualization. Earth2Class workshop archive

TEACHING & EXCHANGE · UNIVERSITY OF ZAMBIA

Field collection, processing and interpretation

Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (HOT) and University of Zambia colleagues shared drone-mapping methods, data management and field practice. June 2026 field sessions and campus products provided material for teaching and discussion.

EFPL + CIESIN · Columbia University · HOT · University of Zambia / NACUAS. The work documents technical exchange and mapping. Learning gains and humanitarian outcomes were not evaluated here.

Field collection

Field sessions introduced equipment, image overlap, survey coverage and the observations needed to interpret the results.

Photogrammetric processing

Overlapping photographs produced campus orthomosaics and textured meshes. The products show an overhead view and reconstructed building and surface geometry.

Product interpretation

Discussions covered data management, processing needs and interpretation. Image texture, reconstructed geometry and independently checked measurements remain different kinds of information.

READING THE PRODUCTS

What did the camera record, and what did processing add?

Working groups

Photographs place people and equipment in the setting of a workshop. They help explain the activity and the roles involved.

Camera observations

Individual frames show the camera's field of view at capture time. Visible-light and thermal cameras provide different observations; the thermal examples here are qualitative.

Spatial products

Orthomosaics and photogrammetric reconstructions combine multiple images. Their resolution and geometric appearance do not establish independent positional accuracy.
