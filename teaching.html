BUILDING TEACHING CAPACITY

From classroom support to field practice.

The classroom foundation

The 2021 Provost Emerging Technology Grant supported Drones and AR/VR for 3D Modeling in GIS, with $19,637 and CUIT support. CIESIN-based teaching connected that equipment and software capacity with SDEV3450 fieldwork, including the 2022 Callicoon exercise and student mapping.

Coursework with communities

SDEV4650, Building Climate Justice, connected field preparation with community-engaged spatial work. Collaboratory funding included a drone purchase. Lamont workshops supported the course's later Staten Island field activities.

A broader teaching setting

Workshops extended to NSF research students and educators, William Paterson University and the New York State Earth Science Teachers Association. Kytt MacManus worked with course teams and colleagues across CIESIN and EFPL.

Equipment support and workshop participation describe opportunities for learning. Learning gains require learner assessment.

TEACHING & EXCHANGE · UNIVERSITY OF ZAMBIA

Sharing field acquisition, photogrammetry and interpretation

Collaboration with the Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (HOT) and University of Zambia colleagues connects mapping practice, drone-data management and field methods. The June 2026 UNZA material brings working groups, campus observations and processed products into the same teaching record.

EFPL + CIESIN · Columbia University · HOT · University of Zambia / NACUAS. These records document exchange and spatial outputs; measured learning gains or humanitarian outcomes require separate evidence.

Field acquisition

Field photographs and campus imagery connect equipment and collection practice with the places being observed. They provide context for discussing image overlap, coverage and the records needed to interpret a survey.

Photogrammetric processing

Orthomosaics and textured meshes show different results derived from overlapping camera images. The campus examples make it possible to compare an overhead composite with reconstructed building and surface geometry.

Product interpretation

The exchange included teaching and discussion of drone-data management and processing needs. Product interpretation involves tracing the source, distinguishing image texture from geometry, and identifying what has been independently checked.

Spatial products
